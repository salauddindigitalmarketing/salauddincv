Google Ads vs Meta Ads for Bangladeshi Businesses

By Salauddin Ahmed 24 November 2026 4 min read

Every business owner asks me the same question: Google Ads or Meta Ads? The honest answer is that it depends on what you sell and where your customers are when they decide. Here is how I help businesses choose — with numbers from my own campaigns, not theory.

Google Ads: catch people who are already looking

Google Ads wins on intent. Someone types a problem into the search box, and your ad appears at the moment they are ready to act. You pay when they click, and you compete on the search page itself. This works best when people already search for what you sell — repairs, clinics, admissions with a deadline, services people need urgently.

The trade-off is cost and competition. Popular keywords in Dhaka are not cheap, and a poorly set-up campaign burns money fast. Google Ads rewards good keyword research, tight ad groups, and a landing page that matches the promise of the ad.

Meta Ads: find people before they search

Meta Ads wins on discovery. Facebook and Instagram are where Bangladesh spends its screen time, and the targeting lets you reach people by interest, location, and behaviour — before they ever think to search. It is a natural fit for visual products, food, fashion, and local awareness.

The reach here is cheap when the creative is right. From my own recent campaigns: 3,951 link clicks at $0.004 per click, 288 Messenger conversations at $0.10 each, and 20,080 video ThruPlays at $0.002 each. Those are real Ads Manager numbers, not estimates.

What the numbers actually mean

A click is only worth what it turns into — a conversation, a lead, a customer. Judge ads by cost per conversation or cost per lead — never by likes. In Bangladesh, customers often prefer messaging a page to filling in a form, which is why Messenger campaigns perform so well here. Set up the tracking first — the Meta pixel and, where possible, the Conversions API — so you can see which ad brought which message.

So which one first?

The simple version:

  • If people search for it, start with Google Ads. The demand already exists; your job is to be found.
  • If people need to discover it, start with Meta Ads. Show them something worth stopping for.
  • If you are unsure, start with one channel, a small budget, and one clear thing to measure — a message, a lead, an order. Test small, then put more behind what works.

Most businesses I work with end up using both eventually, for different jobs: Meta to create demand, Google to capture it. But start with one, learn what your numbers look like, and scale from evidence — not from what everyone else is doing.
