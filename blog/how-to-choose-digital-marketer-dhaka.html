How to Choose a Digital Marketer in Dhaka: 7 Questions to Ask

By Salauddin Ahmed 24 November 2026 5 min read

Hiring a digital marketer is hard because everyone promises growth. These seven questions separate the people who can prove their results from the people who only talk about them. Ask them before you sign anything.

1. "Can you show me real results, with numbers?"

Clicks, cost per click, conversations, leads — with dates. Ask for the numbers behind the claims, not a screenshot of likes. Likes and reach are not results. I publish my own campaign numbers on my projects page for exactly this reason.

2. "Will I own my ad account?"

You should always have admin access to your own ad accounts and your own data. If a marketer runs your ads from their account and will not give you access, your money and your audience history are locked in someone else's hands. Walk away.

3. "How will you measure sales, not just traffic?"

Traffic is easy to buy; sales are the point. Ask how they connect an ad to an order — Google Analytics, the Meta pixel, the Conversions API. If they cannot explain the measurement, they cannot prove the work is paying for itself.

4. "What happens in the first month?"

You want a clear first-month plan: an audit of what exists, tracking set up properly, and one focused campaign — not a vague promise of growth. A marketer with a plan will tell you what happens in week one.

5. "What will you report, and how often?"

Expect a monthly report you can check yourself, with screenshots from Ads Manager or Search Console — not a PDF of vanity numbers. If you cannot verify the report, the report is worthless.

6. "Have you worked in my industry?"

Food, engineering, and real estate behave differently. A restaurant offer and an engineering lead campaign need different creative, different audiences, and different patience. Relevant experience means fewer expensive lessons on your budget.

7. "Can I see your own presence?"

A marketer who publishes, ranks, and shows their own work is practising what they sell. Search their name. Read what they have written. If there is nothing to find, that tells you something too.

Red flags

Walk away from any of these:

  • Guaranteed number one on Google in a week.
  • Selling followers or fake reviews.
  • No reports you can check yourself.
  • Refusing admin access to your own ad account.

The right marketer will welcome these questions. They are, after all, the questions a careful business owner should ask — and the answers are how trust starts.
