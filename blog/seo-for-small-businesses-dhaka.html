SEO for Small Businesses in Dhaka: Where to Start in 2026

By Salauddin Ahmed 24 November 2026 4 min read

Most small businesses in Dhaka find customers through Facebook and word of mouth. Google is the channel they ignore — and it is where people search when they are ready to buy. SEO is not magic. It is a set of practical steps, done in the right order. Here is where I would start.

1. Claim your Google Business Profile first

This is the fastest win in local SEO, and it costs nothing. A complete profile — exact business name, correct address and hours, real photos, and your services listed — puts you on the map, literally, when someone nearby searches for what you do. Ask happy customers to leave a review; a profile with recent reviews beats a bare one every time.

I set up my own profile this year, and the verification alone taught me how seriously Google checks the details: the address, the signboard, and proof that you are really the business. Get the details right the first time and you save yourself a second round.

2. Fix the technical basics

Before writing a single blog post, make sure your website is not working against you:

  • Speed. Your pages should load in under 3 seconds. Mine loads in about 2.3 seconds, and that took deliberate work — compressed images and no unnecessary scripts.
  • Mobile. Most of your visitors in Bangladesh are on a phone. If your site is hard to use on a small screen, they leave.
  • Security. A valid SSL certificate is not optional any more. Without it, visitors see a security warning instead of your website.
  • Structure. One clear page per service, so Google can tell what each page is about.

3. Use the words your customers actually type

Local keywords are simply your service plus your area — the way people really search. Put those words in your page titles, your headings, and the first paragraph of the page, written naturally. Never stuff them in; Google reads like a person now, and so do your customers.

4. Answer real questions with content

Every question a customer asks you is a page Google can rank. One useful page that genuinely answers a question beats ten thin pages every time. Write about what you know from your own work — prices, processes, common mistakes — and keep each page focused on one topic. That is the whole content strategy in one sentence: be useful, be specific, be consistent.

5. Measure, don't guess

Google Search Console is free, and it shows you which searches display your site and which pages get the clicks. Google Analytics shows what visitors do next. Check both once a month. The numbers tell you what to write next and what to fix — guessing does not.

How long does it take?

Honestly: a few weeks to see movement, a few months for competitive terms. Anyone promising the number one spot in a week is selling something else. SEO rewards businesses that do the basics well and keep at it. Start with the profile, fix the technical side, then publish one genuinely useful page at a time.
